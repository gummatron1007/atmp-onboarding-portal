Section Three — Video

Five video formats. Filmed on your phone.

Vertical phone video drives the cheapest leads we generate. The good news: you don't need a videographer, a script, or studio lighting. You need 60 seconds and your phone.

Why this is for you
Every top campaign shares one thing: vertical phone video from the owner.
Homeowners want to see who they're hiring before they enquire — campaigns that skip this take 3× longer to find a winner.

1 · The five formats that work

Tap any format to see real examples and exactly how to film it. Pick whichever feels easiest first — one good clip beats four mediocre ones.

Click any format to expand →

Why this is the #1 format

Homeowners hire people, not businesses. When they see your face, hear your voice, and feel like they already know you — the gap from "ad" to "enquiry" collapses. Our top-performing campaigns are 2–3× cheaper per lead when the owner is on camera versus when they aren't. It's the single highest-leverage 60 seconds you'll spend in this onboarding.

What a great selfie video looks like
The intro"G'day, I'm [name] from [business] — we install [service] across [region]…"
The hookOne specific problem you solve. "Tired of pool quotes that double on the day?"
The proofOne quick number — "we've done 200+ jobs in 3 years."
The ask"Hit the button below for a free quote — I'll come out personally."
How to film it
30–60 seconds Branded uniform Outdoors / on-site No script — talking points Phone vertical One take is fine
Why this format converts

Homeowners can't picture "good work" until they see it on a real site. A walkthrough lets them imagine their own job already finished. Pointing at specific details — the joins, the finish, the materials — signals craftsmanship without you having to claim it. Self-credibility never beats shown credibility.

What a great walkthrough looks like
The wide shotOpen with the finished result so they know the payoff up-front.
3 detail calloutsPoint at the specific things a tradie notices but a homeowner wouldn't.
Brief context"This took us 3 days, finished last Friday" — concrete, not boastful.
End on the ownerIf they're happy to film a 5-second "loved working with the team."
How to film it
30–90 seconds Walk the site Point out detail Daytime light Phone vertical Steady walking pace
Why testimonials beat polished reviews

Strangers trust strangers more than they trust you. A 30-second phone clip from a happy client — even shaky and badly lit — outperforms a polished review video. The roughness is the proof. The audience knows polish costs money; raw doesn't. Authenticity sells, polish makes them sceptical.

What to ask the client to say
The before"Before I called [you], I'd been quoted by 3 others and…"
The choice"What made me pick them was [specific reason]."
The result"Now I've got [specific outcome] and it's [emotional payoff]."
The recommend"I'd recommend them to [target homeowner type]."
How to capture it
30–60 seconds Filmed at their home Unscripted Specific results Phone vertical One question at a time
Our highest-performing format for renovation

The dramatic before/after reveal is the cheapest cost-per-lead format we run for renovation businesses. The visual contrast does all the selling for you — no pitch, no claim, no script. The audience sees what's possible for their own home in 15 seconds. It's why this is our default opener for pool, deck, kitchen, and bathroom campaigns.

The 3-shot structure that wins
1. Before — held still3–5 seconds. Tripod or steady. Worst possible angle of the space.
2. The reveal cutHard cut on the beat drop. No fade. The contrast is the moment.
3. After — same angleIdentical framing as the before. Hold for 5+ seconds. Let it land.
Optional: B-roll5 seconds of detail shots after the main reveal.
How to film it
15–45 seconds Same angle both shots Music in edit Clear before state Phone vertical Hard cut, no fade
Why this format wins the educated buyer

Most homeowners don't know what a good finished job actually looks like. They can't tell a proper weld from a cosmetic one, a real waterproof membrane from paint, a structural fix from a band-aid. When you teach them what to look for, two things happen at once: you become the expert in their head, and every other quote they get starts to look suspicious. You've raised the bar — and only you can clear it.

What a great educational video looks like
The "watch out for" hook"Here's the #1 thing cheap [trade] hide that costs you 5 years later…"
The visual proofPoint at a real job. "See this join? That's what proper [thing] looks like."
The contrastShow the wrong way too. Phone photo of a bad job is fine — the gap sells.
The takeaway"Next quote you get, ask them about [specific detail]. Their answer tells you everything."
How to film it
45–90 seconds On a real job site Point at specifics One lesson per video Phone vertical Confident tone
